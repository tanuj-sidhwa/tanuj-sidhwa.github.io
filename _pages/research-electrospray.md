<p class="research-meta">ISRO — Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre · Research internship, June–July 2026 · OpenFOAM</p>

<p class="research-detail__lede">Electrospray formation couples liquid motion, surface tension, charge transport, and electrostatic forcing. During my research internship at ISRO–LPSC, I worked on numerical modelling of the Taylor-cone and cone–jet regime using OpenFOAM.</p>

## Research question

How do imposed electric fields, emitter geometry, electrode separation, and fluid supply conditions influence interface deformation and electrospray behaviour, and how can these mechanisms be represented in a computational framework?

## Numerical approach

- Studied the Taylor–Melcher leaky-dielectric framework for the coupling between fluid motion and electric stresses.
- Worked with incompressible multiphase flow, interface tracking, electric-potential calculation, and charge transport.
- Improved boundary-condition treatment and computational setup, including interface handling, mesh sensitivity, and numerical workflow management.
- Investigated effects of applied voltage, flow rate, electrode configuration and emitter geometry.

## Contributions and interpretation

My work concentrated on the numerical implementation and on evaluating whether the resulting cone–jet structures and current trends were physically consistent with the experimental observations available to the project. The study reinforced the importance of electrical boundary conditions and emitter geometry in interpreting electrospray predictions.

## Research outlook

Further work involves more systematic comparison across operating conditions and investigating how changes in fluid conductivity and material properties affect the behaviour predicted by the numerical model.
