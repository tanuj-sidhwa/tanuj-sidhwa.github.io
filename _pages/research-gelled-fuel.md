<p class="research-meta">Bachelor's Thesis Project · IIT Indore · Supervisor: Dr. Ankur Miglani · 2026–2027 · Ongoing</p>

<p class="research-detail__lede">My bachelor's thesis investigates the combustion of kerosene droplets containing fumed-silica gellant, focusing on the formation and failure of particle-rich surface structures and the associated exfoliation behaviour.</p>

## Research question

What governs the formation of a silica-rich shell during droplet heating, and which physical mechanisms trigger cracking, delamination, or material removal during the late stages of a droplet's evolution?

## Working hypothesis

Evaporation and heating can concentrate silica aggregates near the liquid interface, potentially creating a network whose mechanical behaviour evolves over time. Thermal loading, internal vapour pressure, and structural heterogeneity may subsequently contribute to local failure and exfoliation. These are hypotheses to be tested, rather than established conclusions.

## Experimental strategy

- Examine the evolution of the droplet diameter and surface appearance during heating and combustion.
- Compare non-burning drying with combustion to separate effects arising from evaporation and from reacting conditions.
- Investigate the influence of heating or drying rate on the morphology and failure of any surface layer.
- Explore the effects of silica concentration, including possible changes in rheology, microstructure, and fracture behaviour.
- Analyse time-resolved image sequences and, where feasible, residue morphology through microscopy.

## What I am investigating

The objective is to connect observable changes—such as the onset of surface roughening, shell deformation, cracking, and exfoliation—to plausible transport and mechanical mechanisms. Measurements and interpretations will be added as they are verified.
